Mahkus' solar + Powerwall home, by the numbers

If you've been following along with my YouTube journey with our system, I thought it'd be a good time to put up a bunch of data to help show you the benefits of solar and Powerwall. This home in the Sacramento suburbs has 12.24 kW of solar panels on an east-facing roof and three Tesla Powerwall 3 batteries. The system was installed in August 2021 with three Powerwall 2s, which were recalled and replaced with Powerwall 3s in July 2026. The home is on PG&E's NEM 2 net-metering program with the E-TOU-D time-of-use rate, gets its electricity generation from Valley Clean Energy, and is enrolled in a Virtual Power Plant program that sends battery power to the grid during emergencies. This page shows how much the panels produce, what the electricity really costs after the yearly true-ups, how much the system has saved and when it pays for itself, and what the Virtual Power Plant events earn.       How it works
      • The Feb payout counts kWh, not dollars: this home is on track to send about 1,110 kWh more to the grid than it uses this VCE year, paid at about 3.9¢/kWh = $43. Every extra kWh used shrinks that by 3.9¢.
      • The summer dollar credit (about $100 left at the end) pays VCE's winter rates: 7.7¢/kWh off-peak, 11.0¢/kWh at 5–8 pm. Off-peak, it lasts longer than the kWh surplus, so the payout reaches $0 first. At peak, the dollar credit runs out first, so the room is smaller.
      • VCE side only. PG&E still charges delivery on every extra kWh (roughly 28¢/kWh in winter), and PG&E's new true-up year started Oct 27, so extra use is not free overall.
      • Totals are net grid use (grid in minus solar sent out), Nov–Feb. The range assumes normal winter use comes in 20% lower or higher than past winters (weather and EV charging). Cybertruck miles assume about 2.5 miles per kWh from the wall. VCE numbers are actual through October 2026.
